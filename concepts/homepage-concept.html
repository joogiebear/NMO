Why we're here

It started with one friend, and a promise.

In May 2015, Nina Mastro was diagnosed with pancreatic cancer. Her friends did what friends in this city do — they showed up, they cooked, they drove, they sat in waiting rooms. And they watched how fast the bills stack up behind a diagnosis.

In June 2019, a group of her lifelong friends threw the first event in her honor. It raised over $8,000 in a single night, and it went straight to a family who needed it. That night became an organization.

Every dollar we raise goes directly to a person or family struggling under the financial burden of fighting cancer. No middle step.

Seven years on, that's still the whole model: friends, a neighborhood, a party with a band and raffle baskets — and one family who wakes up to a little less weight.

How we give

Three steps, one family, every year.

We keep it deliberately simple so that the money moves quickly and lands where it's needed.

STEP 01

Someone nominates

A neighbor, a coworker, a nurse, a cousin. Anyone can submit a nomination with the details of what the family is facing and what the financial need looks like.

STEP 02

The board reviews

Our Board of Directors reads every nomination and selects one recipient for the year. Not every nomination can be approved — but every one is read.

STEP 03

We hand it over

Everything raised across the year's events goes directly to that family. We tell you who they are, what they're facing, and what your night out paid for.

Nominate

Submitting a nomination

Include everything that gives us the full picture of the family's financial need. Nominations are accepted year-round and reviewed ahead of each year's selection.

What this redesign actually changes

The current Squarespace site holds the right information — it just doesn't ask anyone to do anything with it.

Today

  • Pages still carry template names — "Services 1", "Gallery 2", "2017" — so navigation reads like a file drawer.
  • The strongest number the org has, $95,000 to 21 families, isn't the first thing anyone sees.
  • Donating means finding the Donate page, then figuring out which method to use.
  • Nina's story and the families served are separated from the ask.
  • Photo galleries are organized by year rather than by the family each year served.

This concept

  • Five plain-language sections named for what people are looking for: story, how we give, recipients, events, ways to give.
  • Impact numbers sit directly under the headline, where they do the persuading.
  • A Donate button in the header on every screen, and three giving methods side by side with no page hop.
  • The story leads into the ask on a single scroll — the emotional case and the action are never more than a thumb apart.
  • Each year becomes a recipient story, so the archive becomes evidence instead of an album.

Facts shown here — founding year, amount raised, families served, event names, giving methods — are drawn from the organization's own public pages and should be verified and updated by the board before anything goes live. Everything marked "slot" is a placeholder for real content.
